Personal Narrative

I’m Faisal Quaiyyum, a Predoctoral Research Fellow at the University of Chicago Booth School of Business (Fama-Miller Center). I work with Professors Lars Peter Hansen, Yueran Ma, and Raghuram Rajan, contributing to research that connects economic theory, computations, empirical evidence, and quantitative methods to better understand macroeconomic and financial dynamics.

My research is in macro-finance, with interests in asset pricing, behavioral macroeconomics, and networks. Broadly, I study how the structure of the economy (explained through the contracts, balance sheets, and connections that tie households, firms, and financial intermediaries together) shapes whether shocks are absorbed, amplified, or passed on. I am also interested in studying how beliefs, expectations, and informational frictions influence these responses and the risk premia they generate. Looking further ahead, I hope to uncover when differences in financial structure matter for aggregate outcomes and when they wash out, and what our models give up when they abstract from them. Methodologically, I combine general equilibrium and network theory with modern econometrics and machine learning. Across projects, my aim is to link micro-level mechanisms to macro-level fluctuations while maintaining careful identification and clear economic interpretation. Lastly, I am also intrigued by the beauty of theoretical econometrics.

Comfort Zone

Outside research, I read astronomy and follow astronomical figures closely. The scale of the universe continually resets my perspective: it keeps me open-minded, reminds me how astronomically small our lives are relative to what we often perceive, and encourages intellectual humility. It also makes me think about limits—forces beyond our control—and the importance of preserving ourselves and our institutions in the face of them. At the same time, the massiveness of astronomical entities carries an unexpected lesson in contentment: it nudges me to value what is near, tangible, and human, even while staying curious about what lies far beyond.
